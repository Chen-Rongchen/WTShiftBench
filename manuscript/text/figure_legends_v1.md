## Fig. 1. A truth-anchored benchmark defines a pre-specified perturbation-fitness recovery object in HCC38 and HCC1143

**a,** Study workflow and frozen recovery object. A truth-first workflow (perturbation truth → DepMap CRISPR dependency endpoint → frozen bridge object → model recovery adjudication → qualified claim boundary) defines the target-level recovery object before entrant scoring. The adjudicated entrant set includes the shared-mean baseline, GEARS, foundation-model entrants, linear controls, null references and rebuttal checks. The object is unpacked below into three pre-specified components: the truth signal (absolute mean perturbation shift), the alignment endpoint (CRISPR dependency) and the category rule (pre-specified 25/75 joint grid). **b,** Pre-specified 25/75 category rule. Targets were classified on a pre-specified joint grid using the 25th and 75th quantiles of transcriptomic perturbation shift and CRISPR dependency, with corner-defined categories and a retained middle band. **c,** HCC38 target-level joint grid, with n, aligned Spearman rho and Q1 anchor count shown in the panel. **d,** HCC1143 target-level joint grid, with n, aligned Spearman rho and Q1 anchor count shown in the panel. **e,** Grid composition across the two primary contexts. Q2 transcriptomic-excess and Q3 dependency-excess targets were not observed in either primary context and are retained as zero-count categories in the composition summary. **f,** Bridge strength summary. Visual elements are: (i) point, the observed aligned Spearman rho between absolute mean perturbation shift and CRISPR DepMap dependency on target-level pairs, with n = 47 (HCC38) and n = 48 (HCC1143); (ii) vertical error bar, the Fisher z-transform 95% confidence interval of the point estimate, computed in closed form from n and the observed rho and describing sampling uncertainty of the point itself, not a bootstrap; (iii) gray band, the 95% envelope of an empirical permutation null obtained by shuffling the target-to-DepMap pairing within each context 1000 times under a fixed seed, describing the range of aligned Spearman rho expected under the rho = 0 null hypothesis of no target-level alignment. The point estimate, the point-level CI and the null envelope are distinct quantities and are plotted together only to show that the observed bridge strength lies well outside the permutation null envelope in both primary contexts (empirical two-sided p = 0.001 for each context), consistent with a structured perturbation-fitness bridge rather than random association. The bridge is interpreted as a structured target-level recovery object with pre-specified categories, not as fully deconfounded causal proof.

## Fig. 2. Shared anchor tiering separates recurrent bridge support from target proof

The panels are arranged as an evidence-first narrative: panels a–d show that the four final stable anchors (PFDN5, PRPF6, PMF1, ZNF131) are indistinguishable at the structural axes (shift / dependency quantile, recurrence, stability fraction); panel e then presents the per-anchor covariate TVD evidence that separates PFDN5 from the other three; panel f reads out the resulting claim tiers. No asterisks or covariate colour-codings are used in panels a–d, so that the "four indistinguishable stable candidates" framing is not pre-judged before the TVD adjudication. Covariate-exposed cells are marked only in panel e (darker ochre fill with bold numerical value). Tier colours in panels c and f follow one convention: green denotes primary but qualified (stable anchors in panel c; PFDN5 in panel f), darker ochre denotes supporting only (panel f), and lighter sand denotes cutoff-sensitive supporting objects (panel c).

**a,** Shared-canonical anchor ranking. Paired shift (open) and dependency (filled) within-cell-line quantile means for the seven shared-canonical candidates, ordered by the mean within-cell-line joint quantile (mean of shift and dependency quantile means). The grey band behind each row spans the cutoff-sensitivity range of the shift and dependency quantiles. PFDN5 is highlighted as the primary-but-qualified headline anchor. **b,** Low-ink recurrence matrix across the two primary HCC contexts (HCC1143 and HCC38) for the four final stable anchors. Each cell reports the within-cell-line mean joint quantile (mean of shift and dependency quantiles); recurrence is inferred from consistently high values across both contexts, and green cell borders mark Q1-anchor status. The PFDN5 row is highlighted. **c,** Stability fraction across stable and cutoff-sensitive shared-canonical objects. This panel screens only at the structural-stability axis: all four stable anchors (PFDN5, PRPF6, PMF1, ZNF131) share the same green bar and are not distinguishable here; the five cutoff-sensitive supporting objects (ENY2, NPM1, RPS3, RUVBL2, ZBTB17) use the lighter sand tone. Horizontal whiskers indicate the cutoff-range envelope. **d,** Final stable anchors retain high shift and dependency ranks across HCC38 and HCC1143, with numeric quantile means annotated per bar and the PFDN5 column highlighted; no covariate-level marking is drawn at this structural layer. **e,** Per-anchor covariate Total Variation Distance (TVD) matrix across five covariate axes (barcode gem group, UMI-threshold bin, UMI-quantile bin, detected-genes quantile bin, total-signal quantile bin) and the two HCC contexts, for the four final stable anchors. Cells with TVD > 0.25 (hard imbalance cutoff) are filled in darker ochre and bold-typed; cells at or below 0.25 are rendered in neutral gray. PFDN5 (row highlighted in pale green) has TVD ≤ 0.25 across all 10 audited cells, whereas PMF1, PRPF6 and ZNF131 each exceed 0.25 on UMI-related axes. This is the panel where the four stable anchors are first actually separable, and it provides the direct evidence underlying panel f. **f,** Compact anchor claim matrix (conclusion). PFDN5 is the only primary-but-qualified anchor and is covariate-clean; PMF1, PRPF6 and ZNF131 are retained as supporting only because panel e shows they are covariate-exposed despite being structurally stable. Shared anchors support the existence of a structured perturbation-fitness bridge but are not fully deconfounded target-level causal proof.

## Fig. 3. Model recovery is metric-dependent and reveals a backbone-separation trade-off

Across HCC38 and HCC1143, formal entrants do not outperform the shared-mean baseline on backbone recovery, yet they occupy a reproducible non-symmetric trade-off between backbone recovery and structure-versus-context separation. All three adjudication metrics (backbone recovery, shift-excess identification, and structure-versus-context separation) are retained side by side because no single metric is sufficient to summarize model performance under the frozen architecture-aware adjudication; the bounded wording is stated in the final sentence of this caption rather than being embedded as an axis-level claim. **a,** Three adjudication metrics separate recovery modes across entrants. Absolute scores for backbone recovery, shift-excess identification and structure-versus-context separation are shown for the shared-mean baseline, the formal GEARS recipe, foundation-model entrants (Geneformer, scGPT), linear controls (LM low-rank, LM + Geneformer G, LM + scGPT G) and the null reference. The shared-mean baseline leads on backbone recovery, whereas the formal GEARS recipe leads on structure-versus-context separation; the null row is retained only as a visually anchored reference band. **b,** Baseline leads backbone recovery, whereas GEARS leads context separation. A two-metric paired-dot summary contrasts only the shared-mean baseline and the prespecified formal GEARS recipe: the baseline scores higher on backbone recovery (0.81 versus 0.66), whereas GEARS scores higher on structure-versus-context separation (0.43 versus 0.35). Shift-excess identification is tied between the two formal entrants and is therefore summarized in panel a rather than duplicated here. **c,** Entrants occupy a backbone-separation trade-off space. Backbone recovery versus structure-versus-context separation is plotted for the shared-mean baseline, the formal GEARS recipe, foundation-model entrants and the linear controls. The shared-mean baseline and formal GEARS recipe are highlighted as the reference anchor and best entrant, respectively; Geneformer and scGPT are labelled as supporting foundation entrants, and the linear controls remain below the two headline entrants in backbone recovery. A lightly shaded upper-right region is shown only as an illustrative visual aid: no entrant occupies this region, with the shared-mean baseline sitting below its lower boundary and the formal GEARS recipe sitting to the left of its left boundary, consistent with a non-symmetric trade-off rather than a uniformly dominant recipe. This shaded region is not a decision threshold and is not used for scoring or adjudication; no frontier or iso-total reference line is drawn because the present evidence supports a trade-off diagnosis rather than a characterized frontier. **d,** The same qualitative ordering is preserved in HCC38 and HCC1143. Paired dots compare backbone recovery relative to the shared-mean baseline separately in each primary context; the baseline reference therefore sits at 1.0 and the GEARS, Geneformer and scGPT entrants all remain below it in both cell lines. This confirms that the trade-off direction is not a pooled artifact of a single context. Prespecified GEARS sweep variants and the associated stop-rule adjudication (backbone versus shift-excess trade-off, candidate manifest, batch status and training-exemption boundary) are reported in Extended Data Fig. 5; no sweep variant approaches the baseline backbone recovery, and the finite-budget sweep therefore does not overturn the headline ordering shown here. Together, these analyses support a bounded model-side interpretation: `shared_mean_baseline` remains the backbone primary reference, whereas GEARS is retained as an architecture trade-off diagnosis rather than an overall HCC38/HCC1143 primary winner.

## Fig. 4. A prespecified local rebuttal test does not close the backbone recovery gap

**a,** Pre-specified finite-budget GEARS candidates. Six prespecified recipes were evaluated under an unchanged truth object and scoring system: the formal GEARS recipe and five one-parameter local variants varying epochs, learning rate or weight decay. This panel defines the local rebuttal neighborhood rather than an open-ended optimization procedure. **b,** Prespecified rebuttal trade-off subset. The shared-mean baseline, GEARS formal, the five GEARS sweep candidates and three embedding-based linear controls are placed in the same backbone-recovery versus structure/context-separation space. Under the present rebuttal design, no tested candidate crosses the baseline backbone boundary; the best sweep candidate still falls short of the shared-mean baseline by 0.164 backbone-recovery units. **c,** Per-context residual backbone gap. GEARS formal, the best GEARS sweep candidate and the three linear controls are plotted as backbone-recovery differences relative to the shared-mean baseline in HCC38 and HCC1143. All tested candidates remain below the baseline in both primary contexts, indicating that the finite-budget GEARS sweep and the embedding-based linear controls do not close the backbone gap. GEARS training is not rerun during figure production, and persistence of the gap should not be read as evidence for a hidden recipe winner or complete model-side closure. Per-candidate sweep backbone bars, shift-excess identification, linear-control schematic and the full coverage audit are reproduced in Extended Data Fig. 5.

## Fig. 5. Covariate, temporal and endpoint boundaries define the final benchmark scope

**a,** Boundary architecture. Three independent layers jointly define the final claim scope: the covariate boundary audits barcode gem group, UMI signal and detected-gene axes and blocks fully deconfounded wording; the temporal boundary (K562 7d/13d panel) blocks content-level replication and primary co-pillar claims; the endpoint hierarchy boundary blocks treating RNAi DEMETER2 as a primary readout. **b,** Covariate boundary. Mean Total Variation Distance across five covariate axes in HCC38 and HCC1143. UMI-related axes show the strongest imbalances, preventing fully deconfounded wording. PFDN5 remains the only covariate-clean stable anchor. Detailed covariate audit is provided in Extended Data Fig. 9. **c,** Temporal and endpoint hierarchy boundary. Left, K562 temporal comparison (7d versus 13d): 7d shows stronger rank alignment (Spearman = 0.733) whereas 13d shows larger mean shift, supporting temporal stratification rather than monotonic improvement. Right, endpoint hierarchy across four contexts: CRISPR DepMap bridge Spearman exceeds RNAi DEMETER2 in every context (HCC38 0.726 versus 0.276; HCC1143 0.779 versus 0.384; K562 7d 0.733 versus 0.333; K562 13d 0.515 versus 0.300). Full temporal detail is in Extended Data Fig. 7; full endpoint detail is in Extended Data Fig. 8. **d,** Final claim boundary. Structured claim ledger: CRISPR DepMap dependency (HCC38/HCC1143) is the primary readout; K562 temporal panel is supplementary evidence supporting architecture-form recurrence but not content-level replication; RNAi DEMETER2 is a weaker cross-platform sensitivity endpoint; fully deconfounded architecture, content-level replication, RNAi primary readout and mechanism-level recovery remain unclaimed.
