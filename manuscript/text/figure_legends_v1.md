# WTShiftBench main figure legends v1

## Fig. 1. Endpoint-aligned recovery object and resource evidence landscape

WTShiftBench is organized as an endpoint-recovery audit rather than as a direct DepMap predictor or model leaderboard. The benchmark object is a joint shift-dependency space: transcriptomic perturbation shifts are compared with external DepMap dependency strength, and endpoint categories are frozen before model scoring. Resource usability is documented in registry tables, source data and demo files; the main figure presents the scientific object and evidence landscape.

**a,** Evaluation-object contrast. Expression-centric evaluation asks whether generated profiles resemble observed transcriptomic profiles, whereas WTShiftBench evaluates recovery in a fixed shift-dependency endpoint plane. **b,** Fixed endpoint-recovery percentile plane for the primary HCC contexts. Dependency strength is defined as the negative DepMap CRISPR score so that larger values indicate stronger dependency; 25th and 75th percentile thresholds define anchor, shift-excess, dependency-excess, low-information and retained-middle regions. This panel defines the object being recovered; it is not a model output. **c,** Dataset evidence-layer matrix. HCC38 and HCC1143 form the primary model-audit layer; K562 temporal and Replogle CRISPRi datasets provide external bridge-form, temporal, modality and scale boundary evidence; GSE264667 HepG2/Jurkat is retained as a secondary endpoint-extension layer; pathway-focused, non-cancer, gain-of-function, regulatory-element or stimulus-confounded datasets are retained as narrow-boundary, excluded or future-extension entries. **d,** Analysis coverage map showing which dataset groups support observed bridge analysis, DepMap matching, endpoint category grids, model audit and response-program annotation.

## Fig. 2. Primary HCC contexts establish the endpoint-recovery object

HCC38 and HCC1143 are the primary model-audit contexts because they share cancer-cell-line KO perturbation readouts, matched DepMap endpoint mapping and complete model-output contracts. This figure establishes the observed endpoint-recovery object used for the model audit in Fig. 3.

**a,** HCC38 endpoint plane using within-context percentiles for observed perturbation-shift magnitude and DepMap dependency strength. Each point is an evaluated perturbation target, colored by frozen endpoint category; the displayed Spearman ρ summarizes the raw target-level rank association between observed transcriptomic shift magnitude and dependency strength. **b,** HCC1143 endpoint plane plotted with the same percentile and category convention. **c,** Frozen endpoint-category composition across the two primary HCC contexts. Anchor, shift-excess, dependency-excess, low-information and middle categories are defined before model scoring and are not re-estimated from model predictions. **d,** Endpoint-label permutation calibration. The observed HCC38 and HCC1143 shift-dependency correlations are shown against the endpoint-label permutation distribution, supporting a structured bridge above random target-to-endpoint pairing. **e,** Compact anchor claim tiering. PFDN5 is retained as the primary-qualified anchor, while PMF1, PRPF6 and ZNF131 are supporting-only because their structural bridge support is accompanied by covariate exposure; cutoff-sensitive targets are not promoted to primary wording. The anchor tiering governs target-level language but is secondary to the primary HCC bridge definition.

## Fig. 3. WTShiftBench audits endpoint-recovery and common-response profiles of model-generated perturbation shifts

Each entrant was converted to the same target-by-gene model-generated perturbation-shift contract and evaluated under the fixed HCC38/HCC1143 endpoint-recovery object. The figure is an audit-profile summary rather than a model leaderboard: it asks whether generated shifts retain DepMap-aligned endpoint structure, axis-aligned response structure and target identity, or instead collapse toward common/stress-like response profiles. The model-audit regime is within-context, target-observed, pair-observed and endpoint-not-supervised; these results do not establish direct DepMap prediction, broad unseen-target generalization, viability prediction or causal fitness inference.

**a,** Full endpoint-recovery profile across formal entrants, controls and diagnostic references. Metrics summarize total-shift endpoint alignment, axis-aligned endpoint alignment, anchor versus low-information separation, target-identity preservation and common-response dominance. Rows are ordered by model role rather than by performance rank: perturbation-response entrants (scGen, CPA, GEARS formal and CellOT), foundation or repurposed entrants (scGPT and Geneformer), linear or embedding controls (low-rank, scGPT-ridge and Geneformer-ridge), and diagnostic or null references (shared-mean and null). **b,** Total model-generated perturbation shift versus DepMap dependency strength for six formal or representative entrants in HCC38 and HCC1143: scGen, CPA, GEARS formal, CellOT, scGPT and Geneformer. **c,** Axis-aligned model-generated perturbation shift versus DepMap dependency strength for the same entrants, testing whether endpoint alignment is retained in endpoint-relevant axes rather than only in total shift magnitude. Axes are held common within each metric family to avoid panel-specific scale inflation. **d,** Common-response audit contrasting axis endpoint-recovery score with common/stress-like response dominance across all formal entrants, controls and references. **e,** Target-identity preservation, measured by agreement between model-generated and observed target-target perturbation structure, across all formal entrants and controls. For readability, scatter small multiples in b and c are restricted to the six formal or representative entrants; linear controls, ridge controls, the shared-mean diagnostic reference and the null reference are retained in the full-profile summary panels, where their comparative roles are more interpretable than in raw scatter displays. The shared-mean reference is interpreted as a diagnostic shared-architecture reference rather than a deployable predictive model; its total-shift endpoint correlation is non-estimable when target-level total magnitude is constant by construction.

## Fig. 4. External datasets support bridge-form detectability and define temporal, modality and scale boundaries

External perturbation datasets are used to evaluate whether the observed shift-dependency bridge form is detectable beyond the primary HCC contexts and to define boundary conditions. They are not used as evidence for broad model generalization because they differ in cell line, perturbation modality, target universe, timing and output-contract completeness.

**a,** Observed shift-DepMap bridge-form robustness across completed contexts. Points show Spearman correlation between observed perturbation-shift magnitude and DepMap dependency strength; horizontal intervals show bootstrap confidence intervals where available; point size reflects the number of matched targets. HCC38 and HCC1143 show the strongest primary-context alignment. K562 temporal data retain positive direction with stronger support at day 7 than day 13. Replogle K562 CRISPRi essential and genome-scale panels show statistically detectable but attenuated bridge strength, consistent with modality, scale and target-universe boundaries. GSE264667 HepG2 and Jurkat provide secondary endpoint-extension evidence rather than primary model-audit evidence. **b,** Evidence-layer governance map distinguishing primary model-audit, external bridge-form or boundary, secondary endpoint-extension, and excluded or future-extension layers. **c,** Secondary endpoint-extension category composition for GSE264667 HepG2 and Jurkat. Endpoint categories are instantiated from observed shifts and matched DepMap dependency, but these contexts are not used for the primary HCC model-audit claim in this version. **d,** Boundary interpretation matrix. Time, modality, scale and lineage changes are interpreted as boundary tests of bridge-form detectability, not as cross-dataset model-generalization tests.

## Fig. 5. Response-program annotation of endpoint-aligned recovery classes

Pathway analysis is used as response-level annotation, not as target-gene mechanism discovery. Category-level signatures are derived from aggregated observed perturbation-shift vectors for frozen endpoint categories; enrichment does not define endpoint categories, tune model scoring or establish causal mechanisms for category membership.

**a,** Response contrast availability. For each primary HCC context, signed observed-shift vectors were aggregated by frozen endpoint category and contrasted for anchor versus low-information and anchor versus retained-middle response programs; numbers report positive/negative target counts. **b,** HCC38 response-program tendencies from Hallmark gene-set enrichment. Dot color indicates normalized enrichment score direction and dot size indicates enrichment strength or significance according to the displayed source data. **c,** HCC1143 response-program tendencies from the same response-level contrast design. **d,** Cross-context normalized enrichment score map for selected response programs across HCC38 and HCC1143 contrasts. The panels annotate category-associated transcriptomic programs rather than target-gene membership enrichment.
