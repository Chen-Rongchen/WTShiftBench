## Fig. 1. A truth-anchored benchmark defines a pre-specified perturbation-fitness recovery object in HCC38 and HCC1143

**a,** Truth-first recovery object. A five-step truth-first workflow (real perturbation truth → DepMap CRISPR dependency endpoint → frozen bridge object → model recovery adjudication → gated discovery) defines the frozen target-level recovery object before any entrant scoring. The object is unpacked below into three pre-specified components: the truth object (absolute mean perturbation shift), the alignment endpoint (CRISPR dependency) and the category rule (pre-specified 25/75 joint grid). **b,** Pre-specified 25/75 category rule. Targets were classified on a pre-specified joint grid using the 25th and 75th quantiles of transcriptomic perturbation shift and CRISPR dependency, with corner-defined categories and a retained middle band. **c,** HCC38 target-level joint grid, with n, aligned Spearman rho and Q1 anchor count shown in the panel. **d,** HCC1143 target-level joint grid, with n, aligned Spearman rho and Q1 anchor count shown in the panel. **e,** Grid composition across the two primary contexts. Q2 transcriptomic-excess and Q3 dependency-excess targets were not observed in either primary context and are retained as zero-count categories in the composition summary. **f,** Bridge strength summary. Visual elements are: (i) point, the observed aligned Spearman rho between absolute mean perturbation shift and CRISPR DepMap dependency on target-level pairs, with n = 47 (HCC38) and n = 48 (HCC1143); (ii) vertical error bar, the Fisher z-transform 95% confidence interval of the point estimate, computed in closed form from n and the observed rho and describing sampling uncertainty of the point itself, not a bootstrap; (iii) gray band, the 95% envelope of an empirical permutation null obtained by shuffling the target-to-DepMap pairing within each context 1000 times under a fixed seed, describing the range of aligned Spearman rho expected under the rho = 0 null hypothesis of no target-level alignment. The point estimate, the point-level CI and the null envelope are distinct quantities and are plotted together only to show that the observed bridge strength lies well outside the permutation null envelope in both primary contexts (empirical two-sided p = 0.001 for each context), consistent with a structured perturbation-fitness bridge rather than random association. The bridge is interpreted as a structured target-level recovery object with pre-specified categories, not as fully deconfounded causal proof.

## Fig. 2. Shared anchor tiering separates recurrent bridge support from target proof

The panels are arranged as an evidence-first narrative: panels a–d show that the four final stable anchors (PFDN5, PRPF6, PMF1, ZNF131) are indistinguishable at the structural axes (shift / dependency quantile, recurrence, stability fraction); panel e then presents the per-anchor covariate TVD evidence that separates PFDN5 from the other three; panel f reads out the resulting claim tiers. No asterisks or covariate colour-codings are used in panels a–d, so that the "four indistinguishable stable candidates" framing is not pre-judged before the TVD adjudication. Covariate-exposed cells are marked only in panel e (darker ochre fill with bold numerical value). Tier colours in panels c and f follow one convention: green denotes primary but qualified (stable anchors in panel c; PFDN5 in panel f), darker ochre denotes supporting only (panel f), and lighter sand denotes cutoff-sensitive supporting objects (panel c).

**a,** Shared-canonical anchor ranking. Paired shift (open) and dependency (filled) within-cell-line quantile means for the seven shared-canonical candidates, ordered by the mean within-cell-line joint quantile (mean of shift and dependency quantile means). The grey band behind each row spans the cutoff-sensitivity range of the shift and dependency quantiles. PFDN5 is highlighted as the primary-but-qualified headline anchor. **b,** Low-ink recurrence matrix across the two primary HCC contexts (HCC1143 and HCC38) for the four final stable anchors. Each cell reports the within-cell-line mean joint quantile (mean of shift and dependency quantiles); recurrence is inferred from consistently high values across both contexts, and green cell borders mark Q1-anchor status. The PFDN5 row is highlighted. **c,** Stability fraction across stable and cutoff-sensitive shared-canonical objects. This panel screens only at the structural-stability axis: all four stable anchors (PFDN5, PRPF6, PMF1, ZNF131) share the same green bar and are not distinguishable here; the five cutoff-sensitive supporting objects (ENY2, NPM1, RPS3, RUVBL2, ZBTB17) use the lighter sand tone. Horizontal whiskers indicate the cutoff-range envelope. **d,** Final stable anchors retain high shift and dependency ranks across HCC38 and HCC1143, with numeric quantile means annotated per bar and the PFDN5 column highlighted; no covariate-level marking is drawn at this structural layer. **e,** Per-anchor covariate Total Variation Distance (TVD) matrix across five covariate axes (barcode gem group, UMI-threshold bin, UMI-quantile bin, detected-genes quantile bin, total-signal quantile bin) and the two HCC contexts, for the four final stable anchors. Cells with TVD > 0.25 (hard imbalance cutoff) are filled in darker ochre and bold-typed; cells at or below 0.25 are rendered in neutral gray. PFDN5 (row highlighted in pale green) has TVD ≤ 0.25 across all 10 audited cells, whereas PMF1, PRPF6 and ZNF131 each exceed 0.25 on UMI-related axes. This is the panel where the four stable anchors are first actually separable, and it provides the direct evidence underlying panel f. **f,** Compact anchor claim matrix (conclusion). PFDN5 is the only primary-but-qualified anchor and is covariate-clean; PMF1, PRPF6 and ZNF131 are retained as supporting only because panel e shows they are covariate-exposed despite being structurally stable. Shared anchors support the existence of a structured perturbation-fitness bridge but are not fully deconfounded target-level causal proof.

## Fig. 3. Model recovery is metric-dependent and reveals a backbone-separation trade-off

Across HCC38 and HCC1143, formal entrants do not outperform the shared-mean baseline on backbone recovery, yet they occupy a reproducible non-symmetric trade-off between backbone recovery and structure-versus-context separation. All three adjudication metrics (backbone recovery, shift-excess identification, and structure-versus-context separation) are retained side by side because no single metric is sufficient to summarize model performance under the frozen architecture-aware adjudication; the corresponding panel titles deliberately reduce interpretation inside the plots and the bounded wording is stated in the final sentence of this caption rather than inside the axes. **a,** Three adjudication metrics separate recovery modes across entrants. Absolute scores for backbone recovery, shift-excess identification and structure-versus-context separation are shown for the shared-mean baseline, the formal GEARS recipe, foundation-model entrants (Geneformer, scGPT), linear controls (LM low-rank, LM + Geneformer G, LM + scGPT G) and the null reference. The shared-mean baseline leads on backbone recovery, whereas the formal GEARS recipe leads on structure-versus-context separation; the null row is shown only as a reference band. **b,** Baseline leads backbone recovery, whereas GEARS leads context separation. A two-metric paired-dot summary contrasts only the shared-mean baseline and the prespecified formal GEARS recipe: the baseline scores higher on backbone recovery (0.81 versus 0.66), whereas GEARS scores higher on structure-versus-context separation (0.43 versus 0.35). Shift-excess identification is tied between the two formal entrants and is therefore summarized in panel a rather than duplicated here. **c,** Entrants occupy a backbone-separation trade-off space. Backbone recovery versus structure-versus-context separation is plotted across all entrants. The shared-mean baseline and formal GEARS recipe are highlighted; Geneformer and scGPT are labelled for reference; GEARS sweep variants, linear controls and the null reference are shown as a supporting cloud of unlabelled points grouped by family in the legend. A lightly shaded upper-right reference region is shown as an illustrative visual aid: no entrant occupies this region, with the shared-mean baseline sitting below its lower boundary and the formal GEARS recipe sitting to the left of its left boundary, consistent with a non-symmetric trade-off rather than a uniformly dominant recipe. This shaded region is not a decision threshold and is not used for scoring or adjudication; no frontier or iso-total reference line is drawn because the present evidence supports a trade-off diagnosis rather than a characterized frontier. **d,** The same qualitative ordering is preserved in HCC38 and HCC1143. Paired dots compare the shared-mean baseline and the formal GEARS recipe on backbone recovery separately in each primary context; the baseline exceeds GEARS on backbone recovery in both cell lines, confirming that the trade-off direction is not a pooled artifact of a single context. Prespecified GEARS sweep variants and the associated stop-rule adjudication (backbone versus shift-excess trade-off, candidate manifest, batch status and training-exemption boundary) are reported in Extended Data Fig. 5; no sweep variant approaches the baseline backbone recovery, and the finite-budget sweep therefore does not overturn the headline ordering shown here. Together, these analyses support a bounded model-side interpretation: shared_mean_baseline remains the backbone primary reference, whereas GEARS is retained as an architecture trade-off diagnosis rather than an overall HCC38/HCC1143 primary winner.

## Fig. 4. A prespecified local rebuttal test does not close the backbone recovery gap

**a,** Pre-specified local rebuttal design. A finite-budget GEARS neighborhood sweep was evaluated under an unchanged truth object and scoring system as a local rebuttal test rather than an open-ended optimization procedure. Sweep dimensions (epochs, learning rate, weight decay) and invariants (truth object, scoring system, model class) are summarized below the schematic; the full recipe manifest is reproduced in Extended Data Fig. 5. **b,** Unified rebuttal trade-off map. The shared-mean baseline, GEARS formal, all finite-sweep GEARS candidates and the three linear embedding controls are placed in the same backbone-recovery versus structure/context-separation space. Under the present rebuttal design, no candidate crosses the baseline backbone boundary (shaded right-hand region); visual encoding is harmonized with Fig. 3c. **c,** Linear-control headline dumbbell. The low-rank linear decoder, the Geneformer-ridge control and the scGPT-ridge control remain below the shared-mean baseline in backbone recovery, indicating that frozen target embeddings plus a linear readout do not close the baseline gap. Full target coverage was retained for all linear controls in both primary contexts (47/47; see Extended Data Fig. 5). **d,** Interpretation boundary. Under the present benchmark definition and prespecified local rebuttal design, persistence of the backbone gap is most parsimoniously read as consistent with a task-structure or direction-level mismatch, while not excluding other untested model-side factors. Per-candidate sweep backbone bars, shift-excess identification, linear-control schematic and the full coverage audit are reproduced in Extended Data Fig. 5.
